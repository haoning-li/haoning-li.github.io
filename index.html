Research themes

  • 🧩Multimodal analysis of digital media

  • 🎮Game interfaces & interactivity
  • 🌐Media mix & transmedia storytelling
  • 👥Fandom & fan content

I am a PhD candidate in Applied Linguistics at the School of the Arts, Queen Mary University of London, where I am in the final stage of writing up my thesis. During my PhD, I have developed an interest in social media and video games from a social semiotic perspective, including how users interact with them. My PhD research examines how character-centric discourse is built into the interface interactivity of gacha games, which offers insight into how character progression is recontextualised within these games. I am also interested in fandom and popular culture, game translation and localisation, and broader digital media ecosystems—interests that extend to game design, digital humanities and human–computer interaction.

My PhD research primarily draws on multimodal discourse analysis and close reading to examine games. Beyond my PhD project, I have experience with corpus linguistic methods and using Python and R to support literature review and explore textual data.

Before my PhD, I completed a Master of Translation and Interpreting at Beijing International Studies University with a focus on multimodality and interactivity in game localisation and worked as a lecturer at Tangshan Vocational & Technical College, where I taught College English and was responsible for class management and student mentoring. At Queen Mary, I currently teach academic reading and writing in the Department of International Education, and I am an Associate Fellow of the Higher Education Academy (AFHEA).
